1 in 5 visitors now find their room through search

How I replaced a generic enquiry form with a browsable, availability-aware room finder for a London housing charity.

LHA London provides affordable accommodation across 14 hostels. Applicants had no way to see which rooms existed or were free, so the team was flooded with enquiries it couldn't act on. The job: help people find the right room before they get in touch.

Search by area and room type from the homepage, then browse live availability, photos and prices.

01

The challenge: enquiries with no context

UX · Problem discovery

The old site listed LHA's hostels but stopped there: no rooms, no photos, no availability. Applicants enquired blind — often for high-demand singles that were already full — and the small team spent its time chasing details and turning people away.

LHA didn't need more enquiries. It needed better ones.

Before After

Drag to compare. "Enquire Now" became "View Rooms", and a single search box became area + room type.

02

Designing for a vetting process, not instant booking

Competitor audit · Stakeholder research

I audited how Booking.com and YHA — LHA's closest competitor — handle room discovery. Both set the bar: filterable listings, room photography, live availability and clear pricing.

But copying them wasn't right. Stakeholder sessions showed LHA vets every applicant, so instant booking was off the table. And with only a few staff running the site, everything had to be manageable without a developer.

03

Routing every applicant to the right form

System architecture · UI design

The journey starts on the homepage: search by area and room type and you land on listings with filters already applied.

Each room's status decides what happens next. Available rooms lead to a booking form; full rooms lead to a waiting list. Either way, the room, hostel and room type pass through in the URL and pre-fill the form — so every enquiry arrives specific and ready to action.

Full rooms never dead-end: similar available rooms, matched to the applicant's filters, appear right below.

The routing logic: live room status decides the destination; URL parameters pre-fill the form.

Room cards put photos, price, room type and a live availability badge first — the details applicants used to have to ask for.

I worked the card layout out in low-fidelity first, prioritising exactly those four things, and refined it with the LHA team before building.

Low-fidelity mockups of the room cards and listings layout.

04

Handing control back to a small team

WordPress · Advanced Custom Fields

I built a custom ACF interface in WordPress so staff can update prices, toggle availability and add rooms themselves. Since launch, the team has kept the listings up to date week to week — without needing developer support.

05

The results

Google Analytics · Mar–Oct 2026 vs same period 2025

Applicants now browse first and enquire second. Room search became one of the most-visited pages on the site, while traffic to the generic form fell away — and enquiries for full rooms now go to a waiting list instead of the booking inbox.
