LHA London — UX Booking Flow

Taking LHA London's room bookings from a single generic form to a smart, filterable listing experience.

1 in 5 visitors now find their room through search

The Brief

LHA London is a charity providing affordable accommodation across 14 hostels in London. The brief was to overhaul the room discovery and booking flow to better serve both applicants and the LHA team.

My Role

End-to-end delivery: UX research, UI design, wireframing, custom WordPress development (PHP & ACF) and system architecture.

The Challenge

Applicants were enquiring blind

The old website listed LHA's accommodations but gave users very little to go on — no live availability, no room-level detail and no photos. Applicants enquired blind, often for high-demand rooms like singles that were already full, while the small LHA team was overwhelmed with enquiries it couldn't act on.

What LHA needed wasn't more enquiries, but better ones.

The previous site: no live availability, no room-level photography and no filtering.

Research

Competitor audit & stakeholder calls

I audited how established platforms handle room discovery — particularly Booking.com and YHA, LHA's closest competitor. Both offered filterable listings with live availability, room photography and clear pricing, confirming how far behind user expectations LHA's generic enquiry form had fallen.

Copying them directly wasn't right, though. Stakeholder calls established that LHA vets every applicant, so instant booking was off the table — the solution had to work within the existing enquiry process. And with only a few staff maintaining the site, content had to be manageable without developer support.

User Flow

Routing every applicant to the right form

The journey starts on the homepage: users search by location and room type and arrive on the listings with filters already applied.

From there, each room's status decides the next step — available rooms lead to a Booking Form, full rooms to a Waiting List Form. URL parameters carry the Room Name, Hostel and Room Type into the form, so every submission arrives pre-filled and ready to action. When a room is full, similar available rooms are shown below, so applicants never hit a dead end.

Wireframes

Designing the room card

With the logic in place, I moved into low-fidelity wireframes to work out the room card and listings layout — prioritising photos, price, room type and a live availability badge — before presenting to the client for feedback.

Final Design

From "Enquire Now" to "View Rooms"

The homepage search now filters by both area and room type, and the main call to action sends people to browse rooms rather than straight to a form. Drag the slider to compare.

Development

A CMS the team can run themselves

To make the system scalable and self-sufficient, I custom-built an Advanced Custom Fields (ACF) backend in WordPress. The LHA team can now update pricing, toggle availability and manage room inventory themselves, without raising a developer request.

Results

Applicants browse first, enquire second

Comparing Google Analytics for March–October 2026 against the same period in 2025: applicants now pick a specific room before getting in touch, and enquiries for full rooms go to a waiting list instead of the booking inbox.
