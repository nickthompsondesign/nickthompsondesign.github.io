Treasured Possession: Instant Quote Tool

A mobile-first quote and checkout journey with a live running total, giving customers transparency and control over what they pay.

The customer problem: there was no running total, so people only saw the full cost once they reached the basket, and anyone who left couldn't be followed up. I led it from research and order data through prototyping and usability testing, balancing the client's goal of capturing leads with what customers needed. In progress.

01 · Treasured Possession

The Problem

The page promised an "instant price", but the first total was in the basket.

  1. Every field, including two dates, before "Calculate Order".
  2. Item prices showed, but no running total.
  3. People who left left no trace, so the business couldn't follow up.

Goals

More orders · a lead for every quote · fewer drop-outs

Constraints

Fixed budget · WooCommerce · small client team

02 · Asda Design Guidelines · Design system · B2B

Asda Design Guidelines: Supplier Design Hub

A B2B website where suppliers find the specs, downloadable PSD templates and accessibility rules they need, designed around their journey and improved through testing.

Suppliers were moving to self-service, but PDF guidelines meant rejected banners and repeated questions. I rebuilt them as an interactive hub, kept it and the templates aligned with Asda's design system through the rebrand, rebuilt it with the CMS product owner, and tested it with suppliers, which led to the Artwork Checker.

Design systemRebrandAccessibility guidanceWorking with a product ownerImproving after launch
02 · Asda Design Guidelines

The Problem

Suppliers were moving to self-service, but the guidelines were PDFs.

  1. Organised around internal logic, not how suppliers worked.
  2. Account managers saw rejected media and the same questions again and again.
  3. I owned the specs, templates and WCAG rules, so I could see where they broke down.

02 · Asda Design Guidelines

Rebrand and new CMS

I kept it aligned through a rebrand, then rebuilt it for a new CMS.

The rebrand

Guidelines and PSD templates updated to Asda's new design system and UI.

The new CMS

With the product owner, I kept the structure suppliers knew and rebuilt each page from the CMS's standard components.

02 · Asda Design Guidelines

Testing

Five suppliers, one task: find the specs, check accessibility, download the template.

What suppliers struggled withWhat changed
Lost track of which package→Breadcrumbs
In-page links weren't obvious→A "Jump to" label
No way into the PSD toolkit→A video tutorial

02 · Asda Design Guidelines

The Artwork Checker

The finding everyone had missed: suppliers couldn't see how artwork would look on site.

  1. A banner supplied at 1000px could show at 400px.
  2. Asda's roundels covered anything outside the safe zone.
  3. The Checker detects the template and shows artwork at real size, with safe zones.
02 · Asda Design Guidelines

Results

Fewer rejections, fewer questions, and a tool that outlasted the project.

95→98%media implementation rate
~50%fewer supplier questions to the account team
1,000+banners checked at peak through the Checker

It outlasted the project

The Artwork Checker became part of Asda's internal approval process, used before any banner went live.

Checked in follow-up sessions

"Jump to" meant less scrolling. The progress bar meant more suppliers read to the end.

LHA London: Room Search and Enquiries

Search, filters and room cards that help applicants find an available room before they get in touch.

The customer problem: applicants enquired blind about rooms that were full. I led it end to end, from competitor research to the build, and measured the result in analytics: browse first, enquire second.

What this shows

03 · LHA London

The Problem

One generic enquiry form. LHA wanted applicants to see which rooms were available before enquiring, to cut unqualified enquiries.

  1. No availability, photos or filters, so people asked about full rooms.
  2. LHA vets everyone, so no instant booking.
What I designed
  1. Search by area and room type, with room cards showing photos, price and availability.
  2. Full rooms go to a waiting list, with similar rooms suggested.
Before: one generic enquiry form

Asda Pets: Category Hub

A category hub that puts finding the right product first, with funded brand content built in.

A permanent brand takeover was getting in the way of shoppers. I mapped the journeys, designed three options and built agreement with senior merchandising around navigation first.

What this shows

05 · Asda Pets

The Problem

A permanent brand takeover was getting in the way of shoppers.

  1. Sold as a fixed piece of media, with a "skin" over the whole category.
  2. It made the category look different from the rest of Asda.com.
  3. It raised a bigger question: how should takeovers work in future?

Shoppers

Needed the right food or treats for their pet, fast.

Merchandising

Owned the category pages and product ranges.

Brands

Paid for visibility, and needed clear slots to buy into.

05 · Asda Pets

Mapping the Journey

Funded placements as other routes to products, not obstacles in front of them.

05 · Asda Pets

Three Options

Three options, from least to most supplier content.

Option 1

Sponsored slots on the existing category page.

Agreed option

A hub with category buttons, so shoppers go straight to what they need.

Option 3

No category buttons, more funded content, built around Top Picks.
