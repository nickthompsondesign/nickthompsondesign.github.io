Asda Branded Zones

Bespoke brand pages on Asda.com for major FMCG suppliers. I designed and built pages for brands without their own design resource, consulted with brands that had one, and built the templates and training that let the wider team produce them too.

£100K investment secured from Cadbury for a Branded Zone

Overview

Asda sold two tiers of bespoke brand page to suppliers: Simple Branded Zones, a single page with a top module, and Complex Branded Zones, with multiple pages and full customisation. Each one had to meet the supplier's brand goals while still feeling like part of Asda.com.

Design, front-end build, supplier collaboration and design consultancy, plus code templates and training for the designers on my team.

Designing for Suppliers

Many suppliers didn't have the design resource to create a page themselves, so I designed it for them. That started with understanding each brand's identity, goals and audience, then creating bespoke wireframes and designs before building the page on Asda.com.

Every page was built to be responsive and accessible, with alt text on all images and mobile navigation patterns that worked across screen sizes and browsers.

Asda Pets: Redesigning a Category Takeover

The pet category on Asda.com had been sold to suppliers as a permanent takeover. Historically, the Sales team sold it as a fixed piece of media, with a "skin": a background that took over the whole category. It made the category look different from every other part of Asda.com, and it raised questions about how category takeovers should work in the future.

A permanent fixture wasn't the right approach for shoppers or for the business. I redesigned the takeover as something modular that brands could buy into, that felt cohesive with the rest of the site, and that helped the user journey instead of simply taking over the page.

Because the takeover sat on category pages the merchandising team owned, the redesign had to work with them, balancing funded content from brands with helping shoppers find the right product for their pet.

Shoppers

Need to get to the right food or treats for their pet quickly, without wading through advertising.

The merchandising team

Own the category pages and product ranges, so the hub had to keep products easy to find and work within their structure.

Brands

Pay for visibility. Instead of one permanent takeover, they needed clearly defined slots they could choose to buy into.

The redesign changed three things. Brands bought into modular slots instead of a permanent fixture. The pages matched the rest of Asda.com instead of carrying a full-category skin. And navigation came first, with funded slots below it, instead of the takeover coming first.

Mapping the journey

I mapped how shoppers would move from the existing category page into the hub, and from there either straight to a category or through a brand. Funded placements sit along the journey as alternative routes to products, rather than as obstacles in front of them.

User flow from a Criteo banner on the Pet Food category page, through the Asda Pets category hub, to individual brand pages. Pink dashed routes are funded placements.

Options at different levels of supplier involvement

I presented three stripped-back versions, each built from modular slots, at different levels of supplier involvement. At one end, sponsored slots are added to the existing category page with minimal input from suppliers. At the other, a full Asda Pets hub, with or without navigation buttons, needs more supplier content.

Minimal supplier content More supplier content
Option 1

Sponsored category page

The existing taxonomy page stays as it is, with sponsored slots added: a billboard or leaderboard, brand pods, a promo banner and sponsored product inserts. The quickest to set up, with the least asked of suppliers.

Agreed option

Hub with navigation buttons

A dedicated Asda Pets hub. Category buttons jump down the page to a section for each category, each with its own promo banners, so shoppers can still go straight to what they need.

Option 3

Hub without navigation buttons

The same hub without the category buttons, built around a single Top Picks spotlight. More of the page is given to funded content, but there's no quick route to a specific category.

Considering sponsored opportunities

For the stripped-back option, I considered where sponsored opportunities could sit on the existing category page without changing its structure. I mapped four combinations of slots, so the team could see how much could be sold while the page still worked for shoppers.

Considering sponsored opportunities: four slot combinations for the existing category page, in desktop wireframes.

Agreeing the balance

I pitched the options to senior stakeholders in the merchandising team. They brought their own insight into which merchandising performed better, and together we worked through how much supplier content the hub should carry.

We reached a compromise that worked for both sides. Funded placements stayed, but not at the expense of the user journey: shoppers came first, and supplier content was limited so it didn't get in the way of finding the right product.

Brand pages

Each brand icon on the category hub leads to an individual brand page that sits inside Asda Pets. The header is created from the supplier's logo and chosen colour, so every page feels part of the same hub while still belonging to the brand.

Anchor links take shoppers to the brand's ranges, each with a product spotlight and promo banner. Carousels were offered as an optional upgrade, which gave brands a clear way to buy more visibility.

Templates · Training · Process

Scaling Up the Team

As demand grew, Branded Zones couldn't rely on me building every page. The design team grew from two to five designers, and I trained them on creating Branded Zones through training sessions.

To make that easier, I built up a repository of reusable code for complex Branded Zones. Designers could start from proven components instead of a blank page, which made training quicker and kept pages consistent. It also meant we could move fast when needed, including a fast-track Branded Zone delivered in two days.
