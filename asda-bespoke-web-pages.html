Asda Branded Zones

Bespoke brand pages on Asda.com for major FMCG suppliers. I designed and built pages for brands without their own design resource, consulted with brands that had one, and built the templates and training that let the wider team produce them too.

£100K investment secured from Cadbury for a Branded Zone

Overview

Asda sold two tiers of bespoke brand page to suppliers: Simple Branded Zones, a single page with a top module, and Complex Branded Zones, with multiple pages and full customisation. Each one had to meet the supplier's brand goals while still feeling like part of Asda.com.

My Role

Design, front-end build, supplier collaboration and design consultancy, plus code templates and training for the designers on my team.

Bespoke Design · Build

Designing for Suppliers

Many suppliers didn't have the design resource to create a page themselves, so I designed it for them. That started with understanding each brand's identity, goals and audience, then creating bespoke wireframes and designs before building the page on Asda.com.

Every page was built to be responsive and accessible, with alt text on all images and mobile navigation patterns that worked across screen sizes and browsers.

Asda Pets: A Category Hub

Pet food on Asda.com sat on standard category pages: a list of departments and a product grid. The Asda Pets hub replaced those taxonomy pages with a dedicated destination for the category, with brand pages underneath it.

Because it took over pages the merchandising team owned, it had to work with them. The challenge was finding the right balance between funded content from brands and helping shoppers find the right product for their pet.

Shoppers

Need to get to the right food or treats for their pet quickly, without wading through advertising.

The merchandising team

Own the category pages and product ranges, so the hub had to keep products easy to find and work within their structure.

Brands

Pay for visibility, so each funded placement had to be clearly defined and worth buying.

Mapping the journey

I mapped how shoppers would move from the existing category page into the hub, and from there either straight to a category or through a brand. Funded placements sit along the journey as alternative routes to products, rather than as obstacles in front of them.

Exploring options

I designed three versions of the hub, each balancing navigation and funded content differently, and presented them to stakeholders.

Taken forward

Hub with category links

Category buttons at the top that jump down the page, then a section for each category. Funded banners sit within each section.

Option 2

Hub without category links

A simpler page built around a single Top Picks spotlight. More room for funded content, but no quick route to a specific category.

Option 3

Education-led hub

Links organised by need, such as Dental, Healthy Gut and Low Fat, leading to sub-pages like Dental Care. Shoppers choose by their pet's needs rather than by product type.

Brand pages

Each brand icon leads to a brand page that sits inside Asda Pets. The header is created from the supplier's logo and chosen colour, so every page feels part of the same hub while still belonging to the brand.

Anchor links take shoppers to the brand's ranges, each with a product spotlight and promo banner. Carousels were offered as an optional upgrade, which gave brands a clear way to buy more visibility.

Templates · Training · Process

Scaling Up the Team

As demand grew, Branded Zones couldn't rely on me building every page. The design team grew from two to five designers, and I trained them on creating Branded Zones through training sessions.

To make that easier, I built up a repository of reusable code for complex Branded Zones. Designers could start from proven components instead of a blank page, which made training quicker and kept pages consistent. It also meant we could move fast when needed, including a fast-track Branded Zone delivered in two days.
