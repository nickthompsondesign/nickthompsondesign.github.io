Treasured Possession: Instant Quote Flow

The site promised an "instant price" but only showed one in the basket. I redesigned it as a guided quote that shows the price from the first click and saves every quote as a lead.

Designed, prototyped and tested. Launch results to follow.

The Brief

Treasured Possession cleans and preserves wedding dresses across the UK. They asked us to make getting a price as easy as possible, and to turn quote requests into leads they could follow up.

My Role

Lead designer. I ran the research, designed the flow, wireframes and visual design in Figma, and built the working prototype. A developer at Infoserve built it; the client set pricing and business rules.

What success looks like
  • More quote-page visitors completing an order
  • A contactable lead for every quote, even when the customer doesn't buy
Constraints
  • Fixed agency budget for design, build and testing
  • WooCommerce with an existing product-options plugin

The Problem

An "instant price" you had to work for

Customers had to complete the whole form before seeing a price, and the first total they saw was in the basket.

The "Calculate Order" button was really WooCommerce's add-to-basket button with a new label. The surprising part: the form was already working out the price as people chose. The theme was hiding it. Anyone who left before the basket left no trace, so the business couldn't follow up.

The original form on desktop and mobile (scroll the phone).

Research

Three things the research showed

A hidden price, questions the data made unnecessary, and competitors who show a price within two clicks.

With a small budget, I chose methods that each answered a specific question: an audit of the live form, a year of order data and a benchmark of three competitors, backed up by customer reviews and a quick usability test with colleagues.

1. The price was hidden, and parts of the form didn't work by keyboard

Annotated audit of the original form (scroll the image). Numbers match the findings below.

  • 1
    The order summary never showed a price. The "Your Order" panel kept its placeholder text however many options were chosen.
  • 2
    "Calculate Order" added the item to the basket. The label described one action and the button did another.
  • 3
    The picture cards couldn't be used with a keyboard. An automated accessibility scan missed this; checking by hand found it, so every new component was tested by keyboard and screen reader.

2. The order data made some questions unnecessary

Analytics access was pending, so I exported 12 months of WooCommerce orders (338 orders, personal details removed).

The Insight

Three principles for the redesign

Every finding pointed to the same fix: make the price visible, and make the email worth giving.

  • 1
    Show the price from the first choice. Every option shows what it costs, and a running total follows the customer through the flow.
  • 2
    Ask only what changes the price. Required questions before a price dropped from five to two: dress style and value.
  • 3
    Earn the email by saving the quote. Instead of a gate at the start, the email saves the quote and holds the price for 30 days.
The New Flow

Splitting the journey by what's being sent

One opening question sends each customer down the shortest route, and the quote comes before any dates.

The old form assumed everyone had a wedding dress. The new one starts with "What can we help with?": clean a wedding dress, clean other garments, or just a preservation box. Customers see their price before being asked for dates, so the quote is useful even to people who aren't ready to book.

  1. Your dressStyle first, then value bands priced for that style
  2. Anything else to clean?Extras with quantity steppers, most popular first
  3. Returning your dressBoxed and preserved, or bag options
  4. Your preservation boxSized from the dress, with pattern swatches and a live preview
  5. Your quoteItemised total, then book now or save for later
  6. BookDPD collection and a return date, then review

Testing

Testing changed the design

Testing a working prototype with real prices showed where people hesitated, and the client agreed the fixes.

The developer and I built the wireframes into a prototype that calculated real prices, then tested it with colleagues from outside the project, each working through a scenario on a phone or desktop.

Testing the prototype with colleagues.

What we foundWhat changed
Some testers weren't sure which style their dress was, and the price didn't move until a value band was chosen.A "Not sure?" panel helps people choose, and each style shows its "from" price so the total updates straight away.
The plain date picker didn't show what each date meant for delivery speed.A calendar in the style of hotel booking sites marks every return date as Standard (+£18) or Express (+£50), by colour and label.
"Why does it cost £30 to use your own box?" People couldn't see what they were paying for.Renamed "Pack and present with your own box", so it reads as an expert packing service.

Price first, not email first

The client's first instinct was to ask for an email before showing any prices, to capture leads. None of the competitors do this, and it adds effort before customers know whether they can afford it. With around 23 orders a month, an A/B test would take months, so we agreed to launch price first, framing the email as "Save your quote". Every saved quote is recorded in WooCommerce, so the client can see how many become orders and follow up the rest.

Final Design

A quote that feels like the brand

The tested flow, in Treasured Possession's own look, built as a working prototype on desktop and mobile.

I designed it in Figma using the brand's plum and blush, Poppins and a new set of fine line icons, then built it as a clickable prototype with real prices so the client and developer could try every route before build. It's designed to WCAG 2.2 AA, with keyboard-friendly cards, visible labels and price changes announced to screen readers.

What's Next

What we'll measure

It's too early for results. Eight weeks after launch, these will be compared with the same period last year.

  • 1
    Quote-page visitors who place an order, before and after.
  • 2
    Contactable leads captured from saved quotes, where previously there were none.
  • 3
    Orders recovered by the follow-up emails for saved quotes and abandoned baskets.

The new flow also opens up ideas the client wants to explore next: seasonal box designs, suit preservation as its own service, and preservation bought as a gift.
