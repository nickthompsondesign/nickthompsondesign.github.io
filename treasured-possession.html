Treasured Possession: Instant Quote Flow

Turning a long single-page price form into a guided, multi-step quote that shows the price from the first choice and gives the business a way to follow up with every customer.

[Headline result] to add after launch

The Brief

Treasured Possession cleans and preserves wedding dresses for customers across the UK. Their online price form asked customers to complete every field before showing a price, and the business had no way to follow up with people who left. The brief was to make getting a price as easy as possible and to turn quote requests into contactable leads.

My Role

Lead designer: audit, competitor research, user flow, wireframes, UI design and testing. Development by Tim at Infoserve.

The Challenge

An "instant price" you had to work for

The page promised an instant price, but customers had to work through six sections (order type, dress style, dress value, additional items, Express and two required dates) before pressing "Calculate Order" at the bottom. Until then, the order summary on the right showed only placeholder text.

Anyone who left before the end left no trace. The business couldn't follow up with a customer who had shown clear interest but hadn't bought.

Research

Auditing the existing form

I started with a heuristic review of the live form, clicking through every option to map the conditional fields and note where customers were likely to hesitate.

  • The price came last. Nothing showed until every section was complete, so customers couldn't compare options or check the cost early.
  • Pressure in the copy. Customers were told to "ensure" they chose the correct dress value band, which puts the risk of getting it wrong on them.
  • Mismatched controls. Additional items asked for a quantity but used single-select circles.
  • Placeholder-only text fields. The designer and special-details boxes had no visible labels, failing WCAG 3.3.2 (Labels or Instructions).
  • An ambiguous required field. "Delivery date" could mean the wedding date or when the dress should come back.
  • Rules as small print. The 6pm cut-off for next-day collection was a note under the date field, not built into the date picker.

User Flow

Splitting the journey by what's being sent

The old form assumed every customer had a wedding dress. Customers sending only bridesmaid dresses, suits or a preservation box still had to scroll past the dress questions. The new flow asks one question first, "What are you sending?", and customers with other items skip the dress steps entirely.

The wedding dress journey became four short steps, each with a single focus:

  1. Your orderService, dress value and style, with a price on every option
  2. ExtrasBridesmaid dresses, veils and more, collapsed by default
  3. DatesCollection and "need it back by", with Express suggested only when needed
  4. ReviewItemised total, what to expect and edit links

Design Decisions

Each change answers a problem from the audit

Price on every option, from the first step

Every card shows what it costs, and a running total follows the customer through the flow (a sticky bar on mobile, a sidebar on desktop). Nobody has to finish the form to find out whether they can afford it.

Reassurance instead of pressure on dress value

[Final copy for the value band step, and why the tone changed.]

Quantity steppers for extras

Additional items use – 0 + steppers, matching the instruction to choose a quantity, and sit behind "Add bridesmaid dresses, veils and more" so single-dress customers aren't slowed down.

Express suggested at the right moment

"Delivery date" became "When do you need it back by?". If that date falls inside the standard turnaround, the flow suggests Express there and then, instead of showing it as a standalone add-on.

Email capture framed as saving the quote

The email field is presented as "Save your quote so you can come back to it", which also powers the resume and abandoned-cart emails. An optional phone number on the review step lets customers ask for a call back.

Testing

Testing where to ask for the email

The client's priority was capturing leads, but none of the competitors ask for an email before showing a price. Instead of guessing, we set up a test.

Version A: email first

Customers enter their email before seeing any prices.

Version B: price first

Prices show from the first step, and the email is asked for at "Save your quote", before review.

The main success measure is paid orders, with leads captured as a secondary measure. The optional phone field sits in the same place in both versions so it doesn't affect the result.

Accessibility

Built to WCAG 2.1 AA

Every field has a visible label, errors are checked on each step with a clear message next to the field, and the step indicator, price cards and steppers work with a keyboard and screen reader.
