Homepage animation suggestions

Each example shows what your homepage does now next to the suggested version, using your own easing curve and project images. Press "Replay" to run an example again. Slow motion runs everything at a quarter speed, which makes the differences easier to see.

1.Cards fading in on scroll

Your cards are meant to fade in like the rest of the page. Their own transition list doesn't include opacity, so they jump from invisible to visible and only the slide is animated.

Fix: add opacity to the card's transition so it matches .scroll-reveal.

2.Card hover lift

Your CSS lifts cards by 6px on hover, but the scroll-reveal transform overrides it, so only the image zoom ever shows. Hover over both cards to compare.

Fix: use translate: 0 -6px for the lift, which doesn't clash with the reveal. Both versions only run on a mouse or trackpad, so a tap on a phone no longer leaves the card stuck.

3.Press feedback

Nothing happens at the moment you press a button or card. A very small squeeze tells people the click registered. Press and hold each one to compare.

Suggested: :active { scale: 0.97 } over 160ms on buttons, and a smaller 0.985 on cards because they're bigger.

4.Lightbox opening and closing

On your project pages, the lightbox appears and disappears instantly. A quick fade and scale makes it clear where the image came from. Click a thumbnail, then close it.

Suggested: opens from scale(0.96) and opacity: 0 over 250ms with your curve, and closes faster, in 150ms. The lightbox stays centred, because it isn't tied to one trigger.

5.Lightbox next and previous

At the moment the image just swaps. A short fade with a small slide in the direction of the arrow shows which way you moved. Use the arrows to compare.

Suggested: 200ms fade with a 12px slide, using the Web Animations API (one line of JS, no library).

6.Reduced motion

Some people turn on "reduce motion" because movement makes them feel unwell. Your site already pauses the background loops for them, but the reveals still slide and blur. Here's how a reveal looks to someone with the setting on.

Suggested: keep a short 300ms fade but drop the movement and the blur. Reduced motion should be gentler, not nothing at all.
